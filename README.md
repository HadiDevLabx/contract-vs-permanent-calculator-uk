# Contract vs Permanent Calculator UK — Compare Them Honestly

> Compare on net, not headline. A permanent package includes holiday, pension, sick pay and employer National Insurance that a day rate has to fund itself.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

What a £60,000 permanent package actually contains:

| Component | Value |
|---|---:|
| Salary | £60,000 |
| Employer pension at 5% | £3,000 |
| 28 days holiday | £6,460 of paid time |
| Employer NI they pay, not you | about £8,250 |

To match that as a contractor you need considerably more than £60,000 of billings, which is why comparing a day rate to a salary directly always flatters the contract.

Work out the day rate that leaves you level, then decide whether the premium above it is worth the reduced security.

## What this does not cover

It cannot price notice period, redundancy rights, or how easily you would find the next contract. Those decide more contracting outcomes than the arithmetic does.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
